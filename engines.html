The turbofan engine

The engine on the A320: a high-bypass, two-spool turbofan (CFM56-5B or IAE V2500 on the ceo, CFM LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1100G on the neo). Pull it apart, drag any part away, and click it to see what it does, the pressure and temperature there, what can go wrong and what you see on the ECAM. Then start it, spool it up and see why it is flat rated.

Start and spool-up on the ECAM

An automatic start as the FADEC runs it on a CFM56-5B-type engine: the starter turns the HP spool, ignition and fuel come in at set N2 values, the EGT shows the light-off, and the starter cuts out at about 50 % N2. Insert a start fault and watch the FADEC abort. Then advance the thrust lever from idle and time the spool-up.

Flat rating, FLEX and bleed

A turbofan is flat rated: up to a corner temperature (here ISA + 15) it gives its full rated take-off thrust, and the EGT climbs as the day gets warmer. Above the corner the EGT limit takes over and thrust falls. FLEX tells the FADEC to give the thrust it would give at a higher, assumed temperature, which saves EGT margin and engine life.
