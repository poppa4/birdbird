The unit on one page

What every key and knob does, and where the pages are. Keep it beside you while you practise.

GNS or GTN

The GTN 650 and 750 are the touchscreen successors. The jobs are the same, the way to them is different. Useful if you know one and fly the other.

How it works

With help

One step at a time, in the order you would do it in the aircraft. Stuck? Hint shows the keys and knobs to use and what to enter, and lights them on the unit.

On your own

The same task without the steps: only the clearance and the paperwork. Set it up your way, then check your setup and see what is missing.

Find the page

The trainer names a page or a field, for example “the runway length at the destination”. Get there with the knobs and keys. This builds the muscle memory.

The knobs

Each dual knob has a ring (the large knob) and a centre (the small knob): click the left half to turn it left, the right half to turn it right, or use the mouse wheel over it. The dot in the middle is the push. Hold CLR for the Default NAV page, hold C for 121.500.

The keyboard

When the cursor is on an identifier, type it on your computer keyboard. Enter is ENT and Backspace is CLR. After you tap the unit, the arrow keys turn the right knobs: up and down the small knob, left and right the large knob.

The CDI and the autopilot

The CDI beside the unit shows GPS or VLOC, as the CDI key selects. It is a mechanical CDI: you set its course with OBS, and the unit tells you on the MSG page when the course does not match. In flight, ▶ flies the aircraft: NAV follows the CDI, HDG holds a heading.

A generic, simplified navigator of the GNS 530 kind for learning: not every page and function is modelled, and it is not Garmin software or an approved simulator. The navigation data is fictional (Nordvik XNDV, Skarhamn XSKH and Fjellby XFJB). Not for navigation or for operational use: follow the pilot’s guide, the AFM supplement for your aircraft and your operator’s procedures.
