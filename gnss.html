How RAIM decides the protection level

With five or more satellites the receiver has more measurements than unknowns. It solves the position by least squares and looks at what is left over (the residuals). A large residual means one range does not agree with the others.

From the geometry alone, before any fault, it works out the largest position error a fault could cause without being noticed: the horizontal protection level (HPL). If HPL is larger than the alert limit for the phase (2 NM en route, 1 NM terminal, 0.3 NM approach), RAIM is not available and the receiver says so. With six or more it can test each subset without one satellite and so exclude the faulty one (FDE).

So geometry matters twice: a high DOP makes the position less accurate, and a weak geometry makes a fault harder to see.

    Test yourself

    Model and limitations. Training simulation, not for navigation; the route, waypoints and minima are illustrative, not a published procedure. DOP from least squares on the satellites you place (equal weights). Accuracy: 95 % horizontal = 2 × HDOP × σ, vertical = 1.96 × VDOP × σ, with σ = 4 m for GPS alone and 1 m with SBAS corrections. RAIM: snapshot least-squares slopes with σ = 33.3 m, false alarm 1/15 000 and missed detection 0.001 (the conservative values of the original RTCA availability standard); FDE when every subset without one satellite still meets the alert limit; no baro-aiding. SBAS: HPL = 6.0 × σ × dmajor, VPL = 5.33 × σ × VDOP with σ = 2.5 m; LPV needs HPL ≤ 40 m and VPL ≤ 50 m. Turns in still air: r = GS² / (g · tan bank); fly-by starts r · tan(ΔTrack / 2) early; fly-over turns overhead and rejoins with an intercept of up to 30°. CDI full scale 2 NM en route (beyond 30 NM), 1 NM terminal, ramping to 0.3 NM over the 2 NM before the FAF; LPV angular, 105 m at the threshold and at most 0.3 NM. Vertical deviation ±0.7° full scale for both baro-VNAV and LPV (real baro-VNAV displays are often linear). DME/DME reversion uses Flesland, Vollo and Stord with σ = 0.1 NM. No wind, no magnetic variation; coastline and relief as in the radio navigation simulator (Mapzen/AWS Terrain Tiles).
