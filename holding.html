Holding

A hold is a racetrack in the sky that keeps you near a fix while you wait. Learn how it is built, how to pick the entry in seconds, and how to correct for wind. Then drill it until it is automatic.

1 · The building blocks

Anatomy of a hold

You fly to the fix on the inbound leg, turn, fly the outbound leg, turn back and intercept the inbound leg again. Standard holds turn right. The side the pattern lies on is the holding side.

2 · Direct, parallel or teardrop

Entry trainer

Set the hold and your heading towards the fix, or click anywhere around the compass to come from that direction. The coloured ring shows which entry applies from every direction. Press Fly the entry to watch it.

Your entry
    Direct 180°Parallel 110°Teardrop 70°±5° either
    The quick way, right-hand hold. Look at the outbound course relative to your heading. Within 70° to your right: teardrop. Within 110° to your left: parallel. Anywhere else: direct. For a left-hand hold, mirror it: 70° to the left is teardrop, 110° to the right is parallel.
    3 · Keeping the shape in wind

    Wind correction

    Wind would stretch and drift the racetrack. The inbound leg gets your normal wind correction. The outbound leg gets about three times as much, because it also has to pre-correct for the drift in the two turns. Outbound time is adjusted so the inbound leg comes out at one minute (1.5 minutes above FL140).

    The wind settings here also apply to the entry trainer above. Fly the entry again to see the corrected pattern.

    On the A320 the FMGS flies the hold: insert HOLD at the fix on the MCDU, and the aircraft computes the entry, the turn and the wind correction itself. The bank is limited to 25°, so at A320 speeds the turn rate is below rate one and the hold is much bigger than in a slow aircraft. Defaults here: 250 kt TAS, a typical A320 holding speed at medium levels.
