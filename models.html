What each simulator and tool is built on, what works, what is simplified or left out, and what it must not be used for. NORDAP is a training aid next to approved theory and flight training. It is not an approved training organisation, not a qualified simulator and not a planning tool.

Cessna 172 cockpit trainer

Illustrative modelFictional training dataOpen

Built on
  • A general light single-engine flight model, tuned to typical Cessna 172 numbers.
  • An analogue six-pack panel drawn by NORDAP, and a fictional training area around one aerodrome.
What works
  • The instrument scan, attitude flying, VOR, ADF and DME navigation, the circuit and basic instrument procedures.
  • Mixture and EGT, the magneto check, the electrical system and a vacuum failure with gyros that slowly topple.
  • Your own checklist from My workspace, and a debrief after each exercise.
Simplified or left out
  • No P-factor or torque on the ground.
  • No mass and balance: one fixed weight of about 2,200 lb.
  • Gauges and limits are typical values for learning, not certified data.
  • The 3D cockpit is in beta.
Do not use it for
  • Performance planning, real limits or a checklist. Use the POH or AFM and your school's checklist.

DA42 glass cockpit trainer

Illustrative modelFictional training dataOpen

Built on
  • A DA42-like twin-engine flight model made for teaching. The numbers are approximate and do not come from a certified model.
  • Glass displays that follow common glass-cockpit conventions, with NORDAP's own graphics. It is not a product of any avionics maker.
  • A fictional region with its own navigation database.
What works
  • PFD and MFD, radios, the CDI, flight plan, direct-to and loading an approach.
  • The autopilot, ILS approaches and engine-failure scenarios, with wind and atmosphere you set yourself.
  • Your own checklist from My workspace, and a debrief after each exercise.
Simplified or left out
  • Performance is approximate. Speeds and limits are learning examples, not facts.
  • The trainer gives no procedure text. Every school has its own checklists.
  • Waypoints, frequencies and procedures in the database are invented.
  • The 3D cockpit is in beta.
Do not use it for
  • Learning a real avionics unit button for button, real procedures or real aircraft data. Use the AFM, the avionics pilot's guide and your school's material.

King Air B200 cockpit trainer and type-rating example

Illustrative modelFictional training dataOpen

Built on
  • A typical twin-turboprop panel drawn by NORDAP, with teaching models of the system logic.
  • Typical King Air B200 values, for learning only.
What works
  • Engine start, the autofeather test, and engine-failure, fire and pressurisation drills.
  • Power, propeller and condition levers with their detents, ground power and the annunciators.
  • The type-rating page shows how an instructor can be set up from your own POH, checklists and SOP.
Simplified or left out
  • Own drawings and simplified behaviour. The model does not show every system detail of the aircraft.
  • Numbers are typical values and are not taken from an AFM.
  • The 3D cockpit is in beta.
Do not use it for
  • Type-rating training, limits or procedures. Verify everything in your AFM or POH.

Flow trainer and its cockpits

Examples, not your SOPOpen

Built on
  • Fourteen cockpits: NORDAP cockpits with every control named, real flight-deck photos under free licences, or your own picture.
  • Ready-made flows written from public handbooks and checklists. Each one names its source.
What works
  • Drawing your own flow, one layer per pilot, playback, practice from memory and a practice log.
  • A button to report a wrong or missing control name.
Simplified or left out
  • A control is named after the label on the picture you see.
  • Names on the 737, 787 and A350 have not been checked against the aircraft handbooks.
  • The A350 overhead panel is a drawing. The E190 lacks a few controls. The ATR 72-600 is shown with a photo, not a NORDAP cockpit.
  • The Cessna 172 flows come from the handbook of an earlier model than the cockpit shown. The King Air flows come from a public simulator checklist, not the POH.
  • There are no ready-made flows for the A320, the Seneca, the E190, the ATR or the large jets.
Do not use it for
  • A standard. The examples show how a flow can look. Your operator's SOP decides the order and the words.

IFR trainer

Illustrative modelFictional training dataNot for navigationOpen

Built on
  • The same teaching flight model as the cockpit trainers, in a training world with invented aerodromes, navaids and procedures.
What works
  • One instrument procedure at a time, with the next step on screen and a replay of your first mistake.
Simplified or left out
  • The procedures teach the method. They are not published procedures.
  • The score compares your flying with tolerance bands set by NORDAP.
Do not use it for
  • Proof that you are ready for a skill test, or for flying a published procedure. Your instructor decides that.

Gate to gate

Fictional training dataExamples, not your SOPNot for navigationOpen

Built on
  • One whole DA42 trip in an invented setting: aerodromes, charts, frequencies and weather are made for the exercise.
What works
  • Flows, checklists, briefings and every radio call in order, by voice or keyboard.
Simplified or left out
  • The order of flows, checklists and briefings is NORDAP's example. It is not the DA42 AFM and not any school's SOP.
  • No instructor outside NORDAP has reviewed the sequence yet.
Do not use it for
  • A checklist or a procedure in a real aircraft.

Radio drills

Checked against sourceOpen

Built on
  • Phraseology written from the published rules. The trainer shows the rule behind each call.
What works
  • Calls checked element by element, with the correct readback, by voice or keyboard. No account and no AI.
Simplified or left out
  • Call signs, aerodromes and frequencies in the drills are invented.
  • Local practice differs between countries. The drill says which rule it follows.
  • Speech recognition can mishear. The keyboard is a full alternative.
Do not use it for
  • A radio licence test, or as the last word on phraseology. Use the regulation and your instructor.

Briefings

Fictional training dataNot for navigationOpen

Built on
  • A departure or an approach briefing on an invented chart, with weather and aircraft numbers given for the exercise.
What works
  • Briefing out loud, scored on structure, the numbers and how you deal with the threats.
Simplified or left out
  • The chart is drawn for training and is not a published chart.
  • The score follows a fixed structure. An instructor may want another order.
Do not use it for
  • Briefing a real flight. Use current charts and your operator's briefing format.

Route Explorer

Open data, not the official AIPNot for navigationOpen

Built on
  • Open data: airspace, aerodromes and navaids from openAIP, terrain from public elevation models, weather from public services.
  • Each map layer names its source and how old the data is.
What works
  • Seeing a route before you plan it: terrain, airspace, weather along the track and the vertical profile.
  • A link to the official AIP of each country, checked every week.
Simplified or left out
  • It is not an official chart. Airspace, points and frequencies can be missing or out of date.
  • Coverage differs between countries.
  • The go / no-go view compares the data on the map with limits you set. It does not decide anything.
Do not use it for
  • Flight planning or navigation. Plan in an approved tool and check airspace, points and frequencies in the current AIP.

Briefing pack

Your own dataOpen

Built on
  • A fixed order of pages for one flight. What you type is the content.
What works
  • Arithmetic on your own numbers: fuel, mass and balance, performance factors and times.
  • Weather fetched for an aerodrome is shown with its source and time.
  • It prints as a booklet and stays on your device.
Simplified or left out
  • NORDAP does not check that what you typed is right, complete or current.
  • Aircraft figures offered as examples are learning values, not facts.
  • The go / no-go page sums up your own entries against your own limits. It is not an operational assessment.
Do not use it for
  • Replacing the AFM, NOTAM, minima or weather from official sources. Check each of them yourself.

Virtual Labs

Illustrative modelOpen

Built on
  • Simplified physics that shows how things relate. Each lab states its purpose and what it leaves out.
What works
  • Changing one thing at a time and seeing what follows, in six subject areas.
Simplified or left out
  • Aircraft figures, also where a lab says A320, are illustrative values.
  • The labs are a visual reference. They do not cover a whole ATPL subject and are not a question bank.
Do not use it for
  • Operational decisions or exam preparation alone.

What the status words mean

  • BetaThe feature works but is not finished. It can fail on some devices.
  • Illustrative modelA simplified model that shows how things relate. The numbers are typical or example values.
  • Fictional training dataAerodromes, charts, frequencies, weather or call signs are invented for the exercise.
  • Examples, not your SOPOne way of doing it, shown as an example. Your operator or school decides.
  • Checked against sourceThe content is written from a named source and has been compared with it by NORDAP.
  • Open data, not the official AIPReal data from open sources. It can be incomplete or out of date and is not the official publication.
  • Your own dataWhat you typed. NORDAP calculates with it but does not check it.
  • Not for navigationMust not be used to plan or fly a real flight.
