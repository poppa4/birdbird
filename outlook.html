How it is worked out

For the aerodrome's position NORDAP reads several independent weather models: MET Norway's forecast (MEPS, 2.5 km, over the Nordic countries; ECMWF beyond), ECMWF IFS, DWD ICON, the UK Met Office model, DMI HARMONIE-AROME (2 km, Northern Europe, about 2½ days ahead) and NOAA GFS. Each model's vertical profile (height, temperature and humidity at pressure levels from the ground up to about FL300) shows where the air is saturated: that is where the cloud layers are, with base, top and amount. GFS gives its own cloud base and top. Where a model gives no profile, the cumulus base is estimated from the temperature–dew point spread (about 400 ft per °C).

The estimate takes the median of the models for ceiling, lowest cloud, visibility, wind and freezing level, and draws the layers of the model closest to that median. Icing is flagged in cloud between 0 °C and −20 °C; showers and CB from convective energy (CAPE) and MET Norway's thunder probability. The status follows the SERA minima in a control zone (VFR: visibility 5 km and ceiling 1 500 ft; special VFR: 1 500 m and 600 ft). Confidence falls with lead time and with disagreement between the models.

Cloud heights are above the aerodrome; the freezing level and icing are altitudes (above mean sea level). The levels a model uses are 1 000–2 000 ft apart near the ground, so a cloud base can be off by several hundred feet, and more days ahead. This is a model estimate for planning practice, not an official forecast or briefing. The TAF, the official briefing and the rules decide.
