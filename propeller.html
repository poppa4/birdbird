Asymmetric thrust and rudder authority

One engine pushes, the other only drags. The yawing moment is the live engine’s thrust times its distance from the centreline, plus the windmilling drag of the failed engine on the other side. The rudder answers with a side force on the fin, and that force grows with the square of the speed. Centre the beta target with the rudder pedal.

VMCG, VMCA and the take-off speeds

Fail an engine on the take-off roll. The pilot recognises it after about a second and applies full rudder, without nose-wheel steering, as in certification. VMCG is the lowest failure speed from which the aircraft is back on a heading parallel to the centreline without drifting more than 30 ft sideways. In the air the same logic gives VMCA, flown with no more than 5° of bank toward the live engine.

VMCG · on the ground

Engine failure at take-off thrust, rudder only, no nose-wheel steering. The track may not deviate more than 30 ft from the centreline before the heading is parallel again. V1 may not be lower than VMCG.

VMCA · in the air

Critical engine suddenly inoperative, take-off thrust on the other, most adverse CG, take-off configuration, out of ground effect, straight flight with no more than 5° of bank toward the live engine. VR must be at least 1.05 VMCA and V2 at least 1.10 VMCA.

VMCL · approach and landing

The same idea in the landing configuration, with go-around thrust on the live engine. The reference approach speed may not be lower than VMCL, so flying VLS or above keeps you clear of it during a one-engine approach and go-around.
