1 · Understand

Key concepts

Every radio aid answers one question: where is the station from me, or where am I from the station? Each card opens the lesson in the simulator where you see it happen.

Every term in full
Radial (QDR)
A magnetic bearing FROM a VOR. You are on the 090 radial when the station is due west of you, whatever your heading.
QDM
The magnetic bearing TO the station: radial ± 180. Fly heading QDM in still air and you arrive overhead.
OBS and CDI
The Omni Bearing Selector sets a course; the Course Deviation Indicator shows how far you are from it: 2° per dot, full scale 10° for a VOR.
TO / FROM
Whether the selected course leads towards or away from the station from where you are. Abeam, and over the station, the flag shows OFF.
Relative bearing (RB)
The angle from the nose to the NDB, clockwise, shown by a fixed-card ADF. QDM = magnetic heading + RB.
RMI
A slaved compass card with needles that point at the stations: the head reads QDM, the tail the radial.
DME
Slant range to the station in NM, its rate of change and the time to get there. Only right about ground speed when flying straight to or from it.
HSI
A CDI on a heading card: the course arrow and deviation bar turn with the aircraft, so the bar always lies on the side the course is.
Wind correction angle
The angle between heading and track needed to cancel drift: about asin(crosswind / TAS).
Localiser and glide slope
The ILS: a lateral beam about four times as sensitive as a VOR (≈ ±2.5° full scale) and a vertical beam on a 3° path (≈ ±0.7°).
Deep dive · how the receivers work

VOR. The station sends a reference signal and a variable signal whose phase difference equals the magnetic bearing from the station. The receiver compares that bearing with the course you set: the needle shows the difference (up to 10°), the flag which half of the circle you are in.

ADF and NDB. A loop antenna finds the direction of the LF/MF signal relative to the aircraft. Night effect, coastal refraction, mountains and thunderstorms bend that direction; a VOR does not suffer from these.

DME. The aircraft sends pulse pairs, the ground station replies after a fixed delay, and the round-trip time gives the slant range. Ground speed and time are computed from how fast that range changes.

    3 · Key points

    Key points

    1Position, not heading

    A VOR needle and its TO/FROM flag depend only on where you are and the course you set. Turn the aircraft and they do not move. Only the RMI and ADF needles, and the HSI picture, involve heading.

    2Fly towards the needle

    When the course you set roughly matches the direction you fly, the CDI needle points to the course. Fly the reciprocal and it reverse-senses. An HSI always shows the course on the correct side.

    3Wind makes heading and track differ

    Hold a heading in a crosswind and you drift off the line. Find the heading into wind that keeps the needle still: about asin(crosswind / TAS).

    Pilot takeaways

    • Identify before you use. Check the Morse ident of every VOR, NDB, DME and ILS; a station under maintenance can still radiate a usable-looking but wrong signal.
    • Set the course you will fly. Inbound: course TO the station. Outbound: the radial with FROM. Then fly towards the needle.
    • Lead the turn. Start rolling onto the course as the needle comes alive from the stop, before it is centred, or you overshoot.
    • DME is slant range. Overhead at 6000 ft it reads about 1 NM. DME ground speed and time are only right flying directly to or from the station.
    • Homing is not tracking. Keeping the ADF needle on the nose in a crosswind curves your path; hold a heading into wind with the needle offset by the drift instead.
    • Hold entries by heading. Measure your heading from the outbound course towards the holding side: 0–70° teardrop, 70–250° direct, 250–360° parallel, with 5° of flexibility at the boundaries.
    • The localiser is four times sharper. Make small corrections, and remember that behind the antenna (back course) a CDI reads reversed.

    Test yourself

    Model and limitations. Simplified training simulation, not for navigation. Navaid positions, idents and frequencies come from OurAirports (public domain, as used by Route Explorer); the ILS RWY 17 geometry at Bergen is approximate and its frequency is not shown; coastline and relief come from the Mapzen/AWS Terrain Tiles elevation data the site already uses for Route Explorer (about 0.25′ resolution; see Sources). Geometry uses a flat local projection around the area’s VOR (under 0.2° and 0.1 NM error within 40 NM) and one magnetic variation for the whole area (WMM 2025, optional). VOR: ±10° full scale (2° per dot on the CDI, 5° per dot on the HSI), flag OFF within 2° of abeam, in a 45° cone over the station and beyond line of sight. Localiser ±2.5° full scale, glide slope 3° with ±0.7° full scale; coverage simplified. DME: slant range from altitude and station elevation, ground speed from the rate of change. The aircraft flies at constant TAS with rate-one turns (max 25° bank); no instrument errors, no NDB night effect or coastal refraction, no turbulence.
