    How to read the profile

    • Terrain shows only the ground under the route. Altitude line adds one level you choose. Climb and descent adds an illustrative path from your climb and descent gradients. None of them is a procedure.
    • With Climb and descent, the first and last part of the flight (shaded) is flown on a SID, an approach or visually. The tool does not have those procedures, so it does not check terrain there, and the line is dashed. Use the chart. The areas are 10 NM by default (change it under ⚙ Advanced); if you add an approach, the approach area starts at the IAF.
    • Between them, en route, the line is checked against the terrain: red dots mean below the highest terrain in the corridor, orange dots mean less than your clearance (1000 or 2000 ft, set under ⚙ Advanced).
    • The brown area is the terrain directly under the route. The red and orange dots also look at the highest terrain within the corridor on each side, which is what the IFR minimum level is based on.
    • The elevation model is coarse and has no obstacles such as masts or buildings. Published altitudes always come from the AIP and the charts.

    Expected departure and arrival

    Built from the runway numbers: a straight climb-out and a straight-in final on the extended centreline. Not the published SID or approach.

    Data sources and limits

    Airports and navaids: OurAirports. IFR points for Finland (Fintraffic eAIP ENR 4.4, free reuse) and Iceland (Isavia eAIP ENR 4.4, non-commercial use). Norway, Sweden and Denmark are left out until the AIP publishers give permission: add the points you need under My fixes. Map: OpenFreeMap © OpenMapTiles, data from OpenStreetMap (ODbL). Satellite: Esri World Imagery through ArcGIS Location Platform (Powered by Esri; sources: Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics, and the GIS User Community); without it, Sentinel-2 cloudless 2016 by EOX IT Services GmbH, contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2016 (CC BY 4.0); if neither loads, NASA GIBS Blue Marble at low resolution. The relief shading over the satellite map in 3D is computed from the terrain tiles. Norway topo: © Kartverket (CC BY 4.0). Map library: MapLibre GL JS (BSD-3). Contour lines: computed in your browser from the terrain tiles with maplibre-contour (BSD-3). Airspace, VFR reporting points, obstacles and small airfields: © openAIP contributors (CC BY-NC 4.0), a community database that is not official and can be incomplete or out of date. Coastline when the map works offline: Natural Earth. Terrain, relief and 3D: Mapzen/AWS Terrain Tiles; Europe terrain data produced using Copernicus data and information funded by the European Union – EU-DEM layers; Norway terrain data © Kartverket; SRTM and GMTED2010 (USGS); ETOPO1 (NOAA). For planning only. Always check positions against the AIP and current charts.

    Draw the profile yourself, like a page in GoodNotes: climb, cruise, descent, hold, approach and missed approach, terrain and cloud. Tick “Draw on my route” to get the real terrain and the route points under your pencil, or use a blank sheet with your own scale.

    Use “Send to briefing pack” at the top to put your drawing in the pack.
    Example: a finished profile
    1. Set the highest altitude and the distance, so the grid has the right scale.
    2. Draw the departure: runway, climb to the first fix or the SID altitude.
    3. Cruise level, then top of descent. A 3° descent is about 300 ft per NM.
    4. Hold, approach and the minimum. Mark the FAF and the DA or MDA.
    5. Missed approach as a dashed line, with the altitude.
    6. Terrain, MSA, cloud base and tops, and the freezing level.
