Where training time is lost

Three problems come up again and again in ground school and type training. None of them is about the students' effort.

01

Theory that doesn't stick

Students pass the theory exams, then meet the same ideas in the aircraft and cannot use them. Cold-temperature altimeter error, Vmca or a take-off performance limit is easy to memorise and hard to picture.

02

Briefings that differ between instructors

Each instructor briefs a lesson in their own way. Students hear different words, different priorities and different minima for the same exercise, and spend effort working out what is actually expected.

03

Type-rating preparation on paper

Systems knowledge for a type rating usually comes from a thick manual and a list of questions. Students rarely get to operate the systems or answer an oral question before they sit in front of the examiner.

What Birdbird offers a school

Everything below works today, in a normal web browser, on a classroom screen, a laptop or a phone.

LearnA learning path aligned to the ATPL subjects

25 interactive labs: 23 in six subject areas, from aerodynamics to performance, plus two on human factors. Each area follows EASA ATPL theory subjects, and the labs use an illustrative A320 as their reference aircraft where they show numbers.

TeachLabs an instructor can project

Each lab is one page built around a live model: move a slider and the drawing and the numbers change with it. A ground school lesson can start from the same picture the student later revises with.

PlanOne standard briefing pack

VFR and IFR versions with planning minima, performance, mass and balance and fuel, plus printable kneeboard sheets. Route Explorer adds a terrain profile, 3D terrain and airspace for the planned route.

PractisePractice in a fixed order

Gate to gate, structured briefings (TSADMM and T-RIBETS), radio drills, a simulator and preparation for the A320 simulator assessment. The order of things is practised until it runs on its own.

How it fits into a course

Birdbird sits beside an approved course and its material. The same student can use it from the first instrument lessons to the airline simulator check.

Stage 1

PPL, night and instrument rating

  • Labs on meteorology, altimetry, pitot-static, radio navigation, holding and instrument approaches.
  • The VFR and IFR briefing pack and kneeboard sheets for every training flight.
  • Gate to gate, briefings and radio drills on the light twin.
Stage 2

ATPL theory

  • The full learning path as the visual companion to the theory course.
  • A short test at the end of every lab for revision.
  • A320 labs for performance, systems, high-speed flight and flight planning.
Stage 3

MCC, type rating and airline assessment

  • Preparation for the A320 simulator assessment and for checks.
  • A type rating trainer per aircraft, as with the King Air B200.
  • The instructor answering from the operator's own SOP and checklists.

ScopeBirdbird is training material. It does not replace approved course material, the AFM or the operator's procedures, and it carries no regulatory approval.
