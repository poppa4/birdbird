Magnetic variation

World Magnetic Model 2025NOAA NCEI and the British Geological Survey

Magnetic variation for each leg in Route Explorer and the navigation log, used to turn true track into magnetic track. The model is valid from 2025 to 2030.

World Magnetic Model (WMM2025), NOAA NCEI and BGS

Charted variation in the AIP can differ slightly from the model, because charts are updated on their own cycle. For planning on a real flight, use the variation on the current chart.

Regulations and standards referenced

  • EASA Easy Access Rules for Air OperationsPlanning minima and fuel rules in the briefing pack (for example NCO, NCC and CAT operations). The briefing pack states the revision it is based on.
  • Birdbird EASA rules libraryA curated set of references used by Flight Intelligence and the briefing pack instructor: alternates, planning minima, fuel, aerodrome operating minima, approach, VMC and IFR rules, low-visibility operations, PBN, icing and oxygen. Each entry gives the rule ID, a short summary in Birdbird’s own words, the revision it is based on (Easy Access Rules for Air Operations, Revision 24, March 2026; SERA, revision from August 2025) and links to the official text on the EASA website and on EUR-Lex. Entries checked against the EASA text are marked as such. It is not a live feed: Birdbird checks for new revisions and updates the library, and the date of the last check is shown with it.
  • EASA Easy Access Rules for AircrewThe structure of the ATPL theory subjects that the learning path follows.
  • EASA Easy Access Rules for SERARules of the air used in the briefing and radio material.
  • ICAO conceptsStandard terminology, phraseology and procedure design concepts, such as holding entries and approach segments, described in Birdbird's own words.

How they are usedRegulations are summarised and referenced, not reproduced. Rules change: always use the current consolidated version published by EASA, the national authority and ICAO, and your operator's manuals where they apply.

The EASA rules libraryWhen you ask the instructor about rules, the matching library entries are sent with your question, and the answer cites them as chips that open the official text in a new tab. Birdbird cannot read the EASA website while you use it, so the library shows the revision and date it was checked rather than claiming to be up to date. Browse the library · EASA Easy Access Rules

Teaching models and aircraft values

Teaching models

The labs use simplified physics to show how things relate. Each lab says what it leaves out. Use the numbers to understand, not to make operational decisions.

Aircraft values are learning examples

Figures for the DA42, the light twin, the A320 and the King Air B200 are typical, rounded or example values for learning, not facts. They may differ from a real aircraft and do not replace the AFM, the FCOM or the manufacturer's data.

Questions written for Birdbird

Tests and question banks are written in Birdbird's own words to check understanding. They are not copied from any official or commercial question bank.

Not for operational useAlways follow the AIP, the aircraft flight manual, your operator's procedures and your instructor.

Cockpit photos

The real cockpit photos in the Flow trainer are from Wikimedia Commons, used under their free licences. They are resized, and the ATR overhead photo is cropped.

The A320 flight deck in the Flow trainer is a picture rendered by Birdbird from the 3D flight deck of the FlightGear A320-family (Copyright (c) 2025 Josh Davidson, Jonathan Redpath, merspieler, Matthew Maring, Thorsten Herrmann, Semir Gebran), licensed under the GNU General Public License, version 2. The picture (img/cockpits/a320-deck-*.webp) is a derived work and is under the same licence; the scripts that produce it from the model are in tools/render-a320. What the displays show is Birdbird's own drawing. It comes with no warranty and is not a substitute for the aircraft's documents.

The Cessna 172 and Diamond DA42 in the Flow trainer are rendered the same way, from the FlightGear Cessna 172P (David Megginson, Gilberto Agostinho, Wayne Bragg, Juan Vera del Campo, onox, thevirtualfer, Daniel Dubreuil and others) and the FlightGear Diamond DA42 (Emmanuel Baranger, OO ZVY, Jeep, MrZac), both under the GNU General Public License, version 2. The pictures (img/cockpits/c172-deck-*.webp, da42-deck-*.webp) are derived works under the same licence; the scripts are in tools/render-cockpits.

The Boeing 737-800 is rendered from the FlightGear Boeing 737-800YV (GPL, version 2) and the ATR 72-500 from the FlightGear ATR 72-500 project (Narendran Muraleedharan, Donald Belcham, Dwayne Gable, Oliver (ot-666), camelon; GPL, version 3). The pictures (img/cockpits/b738-deck-*.webp, atr72-deck-*.webp) are derived works under the same licences; the scripts are in the same folder. On the 737 the panel bodies, which the model leaves white, are coloured grey, the captions the model leaves blank on unlit annunciators are written in, and two hydraulic pump labels are put in the aircraft's order (ENG 1, ELEC 2, ELEC 1, ENG 2).

The Diamond DA40 NG is rendered from the FlightGear DA40 NG (GPL, version 2), with the G1000 primary display bezel and audio panel from FlightGear's shared instruments (FGData, GPL version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/da40-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence.

The Piper PA-28 Warrior is rendered from the FlightGear PA28 Warrior (GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/pa28-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence.

The Piper PA-34 Seneca II is rendered from the FlightGear Seneca II, with shared instruments from FlightGear's FGData (both GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/pa34-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence.

The Beechcraft King Air B200 in the flow trainer is Birdbird's own drawing, the same panel as in the King Air simulator (EFIS on the pilot side). No picture or artwork from a manual is used. A second King Air, with another avionics fit, is rendered from a FlightGear model: see the next paragraph.

The Beechcraft King Air is rendered from the FlightGear Beechcraft Super King Air by Emmanuel Baranger (GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/b200-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence; the beige panels are made a little deeper so the white lettering can be read.

The Embraer E190 is rendered from the FlightGear E-Jet family (GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/e190-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence. The control names follow the lettering on the model's panels; a few differ from the aircraft (the model writes NO ELEC DEV and SEAT BELT on the passenger signs panel).

The Boeing 787-8 is rendered from the FlightGear 787 family (GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/b788-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence. Controls that exist only as lettering on the model's panels are placed by hand.

The Boeing 737 MAX 8 is rendered from the FlightGear 737 MAX (GPL, version 2). The picture (img/cockpits/b38m-deck-*.webp) is a derived work under the same licence. The overhead panel is brightened; the mode lights on the mode control panel are shown unlit.

The Airbus A350-900 is in two parts. The glareshield, main panel and pedestal are rendered from the FlightGear A350 XWB (GPL, version 2); the pedestal is brightened. That model's overhead panel is unfinished, so the overhead is Birdbird's own drawing (tools/render-cockpits/a359/ovh.py). The drawing is simplified: the system panels sit in the order they have in the aircraft, but the place of each single button inside a panel is approximate and some controls are left out. For exact overhead flows, put your own picture behind the flow. The picture (img/cockpits/a359-deck-*.webp) is under the same licence as the model.
