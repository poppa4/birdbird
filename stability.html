Longitudinal stability and the CG

The wing’s lift acts near the quarter chord, the weight at the CG. The tailplane makes up the difference, usually by pushing down. The further forward the CG, the harder it pushes and the more the wing must lift. The neutral point is where stability runs out.

Lateral and directional stability

In a sideslip the fin turns the nose into the relative wind (directional stability) and the wing rolls away from the slip (lateral stability, or dihedral effect). A swept wing adds a lot of dihedral effect of its own, more as the lift coefficient rises. The balance between the two decides the spiral mode and the Dutch roll.

Flight controls on the A320

Pitch: a trimmable horizontal stabiliser (THS) moved by a screw jack sets the trim, and the elevators on its trailing edge do the manoeuvring. Roll: an aileron and spoilers 2 to 5 on each wing. Yaw: the rudder. All are hydraulically powered and computer-commanded; the pilot never feels the air load.
